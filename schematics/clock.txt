CLOCK: 10 MHz crystal oscillator (74LS04 = U9)  ->  /16 divider (74LS161 = U6)  ->  625 kHz to CPU
=====================================================================================================

STATUS: built and verified 2026-10-22 with R1 = R2 = 560R.
        QD read 1.6 V DC with no load and 1.5 V with an LED on it; with the crystal pulled
        it went to a steady 3.0-3.5 V (stopped), so the circuit oscillates on the crystal.

Numbers in [ ] are IC pin numbers. The crystal has no polarity: either leg can go either way.
Keep the crystal, R1, R2 and C1 tight against U9 with short leads.

POWER / TIE-OFFS
----------------
U9 74LS04 : [14] -> +5V, [7] -> GND, 100nF from [14] to [7] right at the chip
            unused inputs [9] [11] [13] -> GND, outputs [8] [10] [12] n/c (spares)
U6 74LS161: [16] -> +5V, [8] -> GND, 100nF from [16] to [8] right at the chip


CHECKS
------
1. Meter on DC volts at U9 [6]: ~1.5-2.5 V = oscillating. Steady 0 V or 5 V = not running.
2. Pull the crystal out -> the oscillation must stop (otherwise it is running on R/C values, not the crystal).
3. U6 [11] should also read ~2.5 V DC (625 kHz if your meter has a Hz range).
4. Won't start? Try 330R or 1k for both R1 and R2, or 10nF for C1.

UPDATE 2026-10-23: CPU clock moved to 1.25 MHz.
  CPU pin 37 now takes U6 [12] QC (10 MHz / 8 = 1.25 MHz) instead of U6 [11] QD.
  The R6502P is rated 1 MHz; 1.25 MHz is a mild overclock. If anything gets flaky,
  move the wire back to QD (625 kHz): all programs still work, just 2x slower.
  The old LED tests (blink/count/ramtest/ramsoak) run 2x faster at this clock.
